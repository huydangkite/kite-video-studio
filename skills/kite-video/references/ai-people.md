# AI people — Higgsfield

Some beats may show a person generated by AI: a **presenter** talking to camera, or a **scene
person** using the product without speaking. They come from Higgsfield through its MCP server
(`higgsfield` in `.mcp.json`; the colleague signs in once with `/mcp` → higgsfield → authenticate).
The **producer** runs these calls in the main session, because each one is metered and needs the
colleague's OK. The crew only ever sees the finished clips in `inputs/ai/`. Not connected, or the colleague prefers
another tool → `manual-generation.md`: you write the prompts, they make the clips (Google Flow is
the first suggestion for shots without speech).

## When to use a person

- Only when the colleague asks for it, or says yes on the brief sheet ("Người AI trong video").
- Presenter: an intro, a "here's what's new" host, a feature-demo guide.
- Scene person: someone at a desk or on the move using the product, a reaction, a context shot.
- **Customer role (dramatised):** an AI actor plays a typical user in a scenario ("a shop owner
  takes an order in 3 taps"), exactly as a hired actor would. Allowed with the rules below.
- **Real testimonial, AI-rendered:** a real customer's actual words, with their written consent on
  file in `inputs/`, performed by an AI person or their own likeness. Allowed, labelled as AI.

## Never

- **No fake testimonials.** An AI person in a customer role acts out a scenario; they never
  speak as a real customer about their own experience or results ("I'm Lan, my sales went up 30%"),
  never get a name, shop, city or job title presented as real, and never quote numbers or outcomes
  that are not verified company facts. That is a fabricated testimonial (illegal under the US FTC
  rule on fake reviews and misleading under Vietnam's advertising law), not a style choice.
- No AI employees or experts presented as real staff.
- **AI never draws the product.** Generate the person holding a phone or at a laptop with a blank or
  green screen facing away or at an angle, then composite the real UI over it in HyperFrames
  (`hyperframes-keyframes` for corner-pin / perspective). If the screen can't be replaced cleanly,
  frame the shot so the screen isn't visible.
- No real, recognisable people; no lookalikes of celebrities or the company's staff unless that
  person has consented in writing.
- Label realistic AI people where the platform requires it (TikTok, YouTube, Meta all have
  "AI-generated" disclosures); the producer reminds the colleague at delivery.

## How

1. **Character first (Soul ID).** One character per video (or per company, reused). Train it from
   20+ generated or licensed reference images; record the character id in `INTAKE.md` so
   revisions and later videos use the same face. Describe the character in plain terms (age range,
   style, setting) consistent with the brand's audience.
2. **Character speaking a short line** (customer role, dialogue) — Flow/Veo may generate the voice
   with the clip (manual route); the words are checked verbatim against the script after
   transcription. The character's voice is not the narrator's.
3. **Presenter** — Lipsync from the character image or a short generated clip plus the approved
   ElevenLabs voice line (`audio/vo/<beat>-<n>.wav`): the voice is ours, Higgsfield only animates
   the face. One clip per voice line, so timing still follows `audio/timing.json`. Check Vietnamese
   mouth shapes on the first clip before generating the rest.
4. **Scene person** — image-to-video from a still of the character (keeps the face), 5–8s per clip,
   one action per clip, camera move stated (static, slow push, orbit). Pick the model per shot;
   Higgsfield exposes several (Veo, Kling, its own).
5. Download every result into `videos/<slug>/inputs/ai/<beat>-<name>.mp4` with the prompt, model,
   character id and seed in `inputs/ai/log.md`. Verify each clip (hands, faces, flicker, the
   screen area clean for compositing) before it counts as a resource; regenerate at most twice,
   then fall back to a design-only shot.

## Cost

Every generation spends Higgsfield credits. Before the first call of a video, tell the colleague
how many clips (character training, presenter lines, scene clips) and ask once. Draft at the lowest
quality setting that shows the idea; generate final quality only after the colleague approves the
draft of that shot.

## In the brief

`PROMPT.md` §6 lists each AI clip like any other resource (file, what it is, beat), and §4 notes
how the person sits in the style (grade to the style's grounds, same grain, cut on action). The
crew treats the clips as footage (`general-video` for footage-heavy beats; `talking-head-recut`
patterns for a presenter with overlays).
