# Writing for the ear — Vietnamese voice-over

The script is heard once, at speed, often on a phone with the sound low. It must sound like one
person who knows the product talking to one viewer, not like a brochure read aloud. `writer`
drafts with this file; `editor` revises against it.

## 1. Start from the story, not the beats

Before any line, write the **story spine** in one breath, 4–6 short clauses that each hand off to
the next:

> Cuối ngày, đơn hàng vẫn nằm rải rác trong tin nhắn → bạn mở Kite → một chạm là đơn tự xếp vào
> danh sách → khách nhận tin xác nhận ngay → bạn đóng máy đúng giờ.

Each beat of the script is one link of this chain. If a beat cannot say which link it is, cut it.
Marketing spine: a moment the viewer knows → the turn (the product) → 2–3 proofs you can see →
the payoff for them → the action. Demo spine: the situation → each step and what it changes → the
finished result.

## 2. Make the lines hand off to each other

Coherence between scenes is the most common failure: each line is fine alone, but together they
sound like a list.

- Every line answers the line before it or sets up the one after. Read beat N's last words and beat
  N+1's first words together: they should feel like one thought continuing.
- Use spoken connectors, sparingly: *thế là, rồi, vậy nên, còn nếu, đến lúc, chỉ cần, xong*. Not
  written ones: *ngoài ra, bên cạnh đó, hơn nữa, đồng thời, tiếp theo*.
- Keep one subject running. If the viewer is "bạn" in beat 1, the story stays with "bạn"; do not
  jump to "doanh nghiệp", "người dùng", "chúng tôi" and back.
- Callbacks: the payoff echoes the opening problem in the same words ("đơn hàng rải rác" →
  "không còn đơn nào rải rác").
- The CTA follows from the payoff, not from nowhere.

## 3. Sound like a person

- Short sentences, mostly 5–12 words. Vary length: a short punch after a longer line.
- Put the important word **at the end** of the sentence, where the voice lands.
- Concrete beats abstract: an action, a number, a real UI label, a time of day.
  "Ba chạm là lên đơn" beats "quy trình được tối ưu hoá".
- Address the viewer as "bạn", but not in every line; often the subject can be dropped as in speech
  ("Mở app. Chọn đơn. Xong.").
- One human moment: a feeling the viewer recognises, in their own words ("tối nào cũng ngồi dò lại
  tin nhắn"). Not an exclamation, not a superlative.
- Prefer everyday Vietnamese over Sino-Vietnamese formality: *dùng* not *sử dụng*, *giúp* not
  *hỗ trợ*, *nhanh hơn* not *gia tăng hiệu suất*, *xem* not *theo dõi giám sát*.
- English product terms only if the product's UI shows them; otherwise say them in Vietnamese. Put
  pronunciation in `say` (numbers, versions, English names).

## 4. AI tells — cut on sight

Phrases and patterns that make a script sound machine-written:

| Pattern | Examples | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Hype adjectives | đột phá, vượt trội, toàn diện, tối ưu, liền mạch, mạnh mẽ, thông minh, hoàn hảo, đỉnh cao | Say what it does, with a number or an action |
| Brochure verbs | nâng tầm, kiến tạo, chinh phục, khai phá, giải phóng, đồng hành, mang đến trải nghiệm | dùng, làm, giúp, xong, có ngay |
| Opening clichés | "Bạn có bao giờ…?", "Hãy tưởng tượng…", "Trong thời đại số…", "Chào mừng đến với…" | Open on the moment itself |
| Closing clichés | "Hãy cùng khám phá…", "Còn chần chừ gì nữa?", "Trải nghiệm ngay hôm nay!" | The exact CTA, plainly |
| Rule of three | "nhanh chóng, dễ dàng và hiệu quả" | One adjective, proven on screen |
| Not-only-but-also | "không chỉ… mà còn…" | Two short sentences |
| Translated English | "giải pháp cho…", "được thiết kế để…", "với X, bạn có thể…" | Active, direct |
| Empty "một cách" | "một cách nhanh chóng", "một cách dễ dàng" | "nhanh", "dễ" |
| Rhetorical question chains | three questions in a row | At most one question per film |
| Exclamation marks | more than one per film | Let the voice carry it |

## 5. Read-aloud test

Read every line aloud at ~2.5 words/s (Vietnamese) with a stopwatch:
- Any line you stumble on, rewrite.
- Any line that needs a breath in the middle, split.
- Any line whose meaning you only get on the second read, simplify.
- Two lines in a row with the same rhythm or the same opening word, change one.

## 6. Must stay true

Style never changes facts: product names, numbers, UI labels, CTA wording and anything in
`notes.md` "must not say" stay exactly as approved. When a fact makes a line clunky, rewrite around
the fact, never the fact.
